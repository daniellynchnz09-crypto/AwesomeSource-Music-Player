MUSIC ORGANIZATION:
Currently in this folder there is a build for an application that scans through a folder for music, and then searches on Music Brains to find the details and cover art for each track/album and applies those detials to music tracks that do not have those details. This system was built to help my previous music player better identify music tracks that I have in my library. But since we are now building a new music player, an improved variation of this existing software will now be directly intergrated into the new music player app.

The main issue that the current Music organization program has is that it copies it's information directly from Music Brains with a fixed parsing logic that does not allow for the mispellings, inconsistent formatting and human error that exists on music Brains. I propose a new system where the information is looked up in Music Brains and then a LLM takes a look at the music Brains data based on what can be found online + the actual files themselves in order to get a grounding on what the detials are most likley to be. As what I found recently with this process is that if I get Music Brains to scan the album and then get the claude model that I was using to build the project to double check the album, the claude model would get it right every time based on the information from music brains and the files themselves.

Another particular issue in music organization is the fact that not every music format can have detials added to it. Most music formats like ma4 and mp3 can have detials added to them like album, artist, cover art, album artist, year, studio etc. But higher quality formats like .wav cannot. I propose that for these higher quality formats that do not have metadata support we add a file into the program that lists the name of every .wav or higher quality format that does not have metadata support in the file. Under each name there will be the metadata detials for each of the files. That way the program can get metadata information for the higher quality formats like album image, artist etc. without trying to read the non exisiting metadata on the file and will instead base it's informaiton off the list.

Currently on my music player I have two main libraries of music that are all mixed in together, the first is my orchestral library that is filled up with a bunch of classical music and movie soundtracks, and my second library is filled with EDM. I would like there to be a feature in this new music player were I would be able to separate the two libraries so that they exist in different libraries of the application with a separate like/rate system. That way I don't have to scroll through a bunch of classical music to get to my EDM and vice a versa. So a system that is able to automatically detect the differences between these two libraries would need to be built.

Each different type of music has a particluar titling methodology that could trip up a future organziational system. Let's first go through the conventions for classical music.

Classical music in the library is typically only orginally composed by a handfull of famous classic artists eg. Mozart, Beetohven, Chopin, Bach, Johan Straus, Grieg, Tiachovsky, Shurbet etc. But the artists that are given to the tracks are often the people who performed them rather than composed them. because of this, the majority of classical albums in my collection are collections of pieces by a particular composer or period in time, or just various classical pieces that are composed by a single orchestra or are a "best of" set containing multiple composers from different periods. I don't want to remove the compliation albums or the information about the perfromers, but I would like some part of the meta data to mention the orginal composer so that I can see all the mozart/beetoven etc. tracks that I have and see what variations I have of each piece performed by a different orchestra. Classical Music titles are also written in some format that I don't really understand, they have speeds, keys, RV(then numbers), versions, revisions etc. This should make it easy enough for the system to understand exactly what music track it is and people who really know and undertsand classic music, but I have no clue. At one point I tried to find the unique part of each track and delete everything else to make a title that I could understand, but as a result those titles that I edited that are now somewhere in my music collection are likley a lot harder to catergorize. In that case we might need to add some sort of Shazam like intergration to find out what those music pieces actually are for catergorization. But generally for the title I would like it if the title is renamed to something that would be simpler for me to idenify on a glance than the giant word salad that normally is classical music titles. But I would also like the original titles kept somewhere for catogoriation and system lookup purposes perhaps in a list document similar to what we plan to use for adding metadata to the higher qualtiy formats.

EDM music in the library typically follows the same titling conventions. for a single track that is made by a sigle artist it tends to be (artist) - (Track name), eg. Eptic - Octane. For a track that is made with two artists the format tends to be (artist) & (artist) - (track name), eg. Zomboy and Eptic - Bop It. In the case of a track that has more than two artist the artists tend to be listed with commas finishing with an & for example: (artist), (artist), (artist) - (track title) eg. Virtual Riot, Barely Alive, PhaseOne & Myro - Rampage. Some tracks are VIPs or Variation In Production, which are essentially the same tracks but made differently, often a year or so after the original track was made. These have the following titling convention: (artist) - (track Name) VIP eg. Herobust - Blockbuster VIP. If there is a collaboration (two or more artists making the same track), that is given a variation in production (VIP) but only one of the artists made the variation in production then the format is as follows (artist) & (artist) - (Track Name) ((VIP artist) VIP) eg. Excision and Space Laces - Cursaders (Space Laces VIP). Remixes are formated in a similar way to specialized VIPs with (orginal artist) - (track name) ((remix artist) remix) eg. SVDDEN DEATH & Yakz - Rock Like This (Oddprophet Remix). sometimes multiple artists remix a track and that is titled similary to how collaborations are formatted with a list ending in an &: (orignal artist) - (Track Name) ((Remix Artist), (Remix Artist) & (Remix Artist) Remix) eg. SVDDEN DEATH - Rings of Pluto (Blankface & Decimate Remix). Because EDM tracks have multiple artists that don't often belong to a collective like a band the artist data point has to be intepreted differently. For example in a collaboration if there are two artists, instead of the artist being called Zomboy and Eptic, instead the track would be put invividually under zomboy and under eptic. For remixes, the artist will default to the person who made the remix and the original artist will be set as the composer. If multiple people made a remix, then each remix artist will have the track added separatley like collabarations. VIPS will always be credited with their original artists unless it's an (artist) VIP format in which the VIP artist will get the artist credit and the original artists will get the composer credit. In albums there can be collaboarations with other artists, and there can also be collaboration albums that are put together by a studio to demonstrate their signed artists. eg. Never Say Die Volume 4. In the case of a collaboration album, all artists are unique, therefore it is impossible to classify the album just based on the artists alone.

sometimes songs might start with 01, 02, 03, 04. This is not a part of the title but is rather an indication of what track number it is in the album that it belongs to.

Also invidual tracks in an album can sometimes have unique album art compared to the album art itself

When new tracks are added to the player, the system will organize them, and during that process a loading bar will appear at the top of the app. once the organization process is complete, the new tracks are added to the library and any tracks that they system are unsure about are added to a flagged menu where they can be verified with a chatbot and given correct metadata. ideally the orgnaization system should be accurate enough that hardly any new tracks are sent to the flagged menu. If a track or album is added to the library that is inncorect and was not flagged by the system, there is an option where the user can manually flag it.
